Abstract	The aim of AMPLIFY PORTABLE is to deliver a suite of light tools to assist artists in terms of creation and experiences using audio-visual information and multimodal sensing of the user and context. This deliverable presents the system architecture for AMPLIFY PORTABLE and the methodology followed that helped to define it. A range of inputs to the system, including physiological, behavioural, environmental and context, are captured for artificial intelligence (AI)-processing to support participant engagement and experience creation. Through unimodal and multimodal processing, the AI element of the system uses the inputs to understand the participants in the different use cases. The AI-based decisions are used in the output block to implement and generate the required outputs and functionality, such as graphic music notation, as determined through a user-centred process, facilitating bespoke and personalised phygital social experiences.
Keywords	Data capture, unimodal and multimodal analysis, artificial intelligence, graphic music notation

Executive summary (Ronan)
Deliverable D3.1 2 presents  the AMPLIFY PORTABLE architecture. AMPLIFY PORTABLE is an AI-driven tool designed to provide video and audio auto-mixing mechanisms along with enhanced understanding and phygital (physical-digital) engagement of artists and audiences through multimodal sensing capabilities and data processing techniques. The AMPLIFY PORTABLE architecture includes several components that provide the necessary functionality to implement the following:
•	Capture, record, deliver and render audio/video using quality adaptation to address network and device constraints.
•	Use artificial intelligence (AI) algorithms to process audio/video streams, automate mixing/production and provide novel experiences.
•	Capture a range of different user, context and environment modalities, including physiological and behavioural based metrics, to understand in an empathic manner the different participants in the AMPLIFY PORTABLE use cases.
•	Enable artist-to-artist and audience-to-artist interaction, taking care of the orchestration and synchronisation requirements.
The functionality highlighted above is achieved by the following in the architecture: 
•	Multimodal data capture: Using cameras, microphones and different sensor types, data is captured from participants in the form of audio, video, movement and physiological inputs.
•	Processing and AI-based decision making: The captured data is used to detect, for example, crowd sentiment and movement parameters (from video), pitch and temperament (from speech), music analysis (from audio) and engagement (from physiological). 
•	Output visualisation: Based on the decisions and predictions made by the AI algorithms, a form of music notation, which reflects an individual’s or the audience sentiment, is generated for musicians to respond to. Other visualisations include changing light patterns that reflect levels of engagement or movement.
•	Audio-video management: Application of tools and techniques to enable the creation of pleasant and coherent phygital experiences for audiences and artists integrated in the spaces where they take place.

1	Introduction (Ronan)
This deliverable presents the system architecture  for AMPLIFY PORTABLE. The architecture description is presented in two stages, firstly, a high-level overview and block diagram, and secondly, a detailed description of all the different components in the block diagram. How the architecture relates to Pilot 1 (Renewing Tradition) and Pilot 2 (Music for Babies) is also explained.
The AMPLIFY PORTABLE architecture design followed a human-centred co-design methodology as described in deliverable D2.1, Ethical and Human-Centric Framework. Central to the architecture design process was the involvement of the Pilot 1 and Pilot 2 leaders and key stakeholders Fèisean nan Gàidheal (FEI) and Cooperativa Paulo Lameiro (CPL), respectively. The focus of the architecture design for AMPLIFY PORTABLE was to develop an architecture driven by the stakeholders in both Pilot 1 and Pilot 2, with the emphasis on their user requirements, and not by the technology partners. The result is an adaptable architecture that can benefit cultural and creative industries.
The rest of this deliverable document is structured as follows. Section 2 describes the methodology and the different stages in the creation of the AMPLIFY PORTABLE architecture. This is followed in Section 3 with the architecture block diagram and a high-level overview. A short description of the individual technological components in the block diagram components are described in detail in Section 4 and this is followed in Section 5 with an explanation of how the architecture components relate to Pilot 1 and Pilot 2. Final conclusions are presented in Section 6.

3.1	Babies & Families (CWI/TUS/SAL/VICOM)
COMPONENT NAME	BABIES & FAMILIES
Responsible partner(s)	CWI, TUS, SAL, VICOM
Use cases	CPL
Description	Capturing audio, video, movement and physiological data from babies and their parents/guardians. Cameras, microphones, smart mattresses, wearable sensors and sound objects are all deployed in a non-intrusive fashion so as not to distract from the performance.

TUS Contribution - Audience Pose Engagement Analysis:
The TUS contribution to Babies & Families focuses on real-time video-based engagement estimation of audience members (parents/guardians and families). The system processes live video feeds to extract anonymised skeletal keypoint data and classify engagement behaviours.

Data Collected:
• Video Data: Live RGB video frames (720p/1080p) from venue cameras - processed in real-time and NOT stored
• Skeletal Keypoints: 543 anonymised 3D coordinates per person extracted via MediaPipe Holistic:
  - Body Pose: 33 keypoints (shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles)
  - Facial Landmarks: 468 geometric points (head orientation, NOT facial recognition)
  - Hand Landmarks: 42 points (gesture detection, e.g., clapping)
• Temporal Sequences: 64-frame windows (~2 seconds) for motion pattern analysis

Privacy by Design:
• Raw video frames are discarded immediately after keypoint extraction
• No facial images, biometric templates, or identifiable features are retained
• Keypoint data cannot be reverse-engineered to reconstruct identities
• Session-only tracking IDs reset when system restarts
• On-premises processing only - no cloud transmission required

3.4	AI-based Decision Making (SAL/TUS)
COMPONENT NAME	AI-based DECISION MAKING
Responsible partner(s)	SAL, TUS
Use cases	CPL, FEI
Description	AI algorithms process the different input streams individually (unimodal) and in different combinations (multimodal) to make classifications and predictions based on data gathered from the Babies & Families and Musicians blocks.
For the children and parents/guardians participating in a concert performance, facial images captured from video can be used to classify/predict emotion of individuals and the overall sentiment of the audience. Crowd parameters such as movement, engagement can also be characterised from audio and video. Physiological signals can be used to predict levels of comfort and explore correlations between the reactions of babies and guardians, and movement and music tempo.
For the musicians in an online music lesson, both the audio coming from the microphones and the video coming from the cameras is analysed. The position and movement of the fingers on the musical instrument and the body position and posture can be analysed, which can be used by the tutor to provide feedback to the student. Also, a comparison can be made between the student’s playing of a piece and the tutor’s playing of the same piece to check accuracy of tempo etc.

TUS Contribution - Binary Engagement Classification System:

The TUS AI-based decision making component implements a four-stage processing pipeline for real-time audience engagement classification:

Stage 1: Person Detection
• Technology: YOLOv8n (Ultralytics)
• Purpose: Detect bounding boxes around each person in video frame
• Output: Bounding box coordinates (x, y, width, height) per person
• Multi-person: Supports up to ~20 simultaneous detections with persistent tracking IDs

Stage 2: Pose Estimation
• Technology: MediaPipe Holistic (Google)
• Purpose: Extract 543 skeletal keypoints from each detected person
• Output: Normalised 3D coordinates (x, y, z) for body, face, and hand landmarks
• Anonymisation: Original pixel data discarded - only geometric skeleton retained

Stage 3: Temporal Engagement Classification
• Technology: Temporal Transformer Neural Network (custom architecture)
• Architecture: 4-layer Transformer encoder with 8 attention heads, 256 dimensions, dropout 0.3
• Input: 64-frame sequence of 543 keypoints per person (temporal buffer ~2 seconds)
• Output: Binary engagement probability score (0.0 = disengaged, 1.0 = engaged)
• Training: Kinetics-700 public dataset, 43,981 videos, 54 action classes mapped to binary
• Validation Accuracy: 82.75% (best at epoch 38/50)
• Training Infrastructure: 12× Tesla T4 GPUs, 50 epochs, ~42 seconds/epoch

Stage 4: Aggregation and Streaming
• Technology: Redis (in-memory data store)
• Processing: Calculates mean engagement score across all detected persons
• Output: Single crowd-average engagement score (0.0-1.0)
• Transmission: Published to Redis pub/sub channel at 1 Hz (1 message/second)
• Latency: <2 seconds end-to-end from video capture to score output

Engaged Behaviour Recognition (42 classes):
• Applause & Clapping: applauding, clapping
• Dancing: 16 styles including breakdancing, salsa, tango, country line dancing
• Cheering: cheerleading, headbanging, crowd surfing
• Singing: singing along to performance

3.10	Communication Backbone (CWI)
COMPONENT NAME	COMMUNICATION BACKBONE
Responsible partner(s)	CWI
Use cases	CPL, FEI
Description	The communication backbone is responsible for relaying real-time data between components of the system in a non-blocking and performant manner. In essence, this takes the shape of a centralised publish-subscribe service, where data-producers (e.g. sound objects, data processing algorithms) publish real-time data to different channels and interested data-consumers (e.g. output devices) can subscribe to these channels to receive continuous updates. The consumers can make use of the data themselves (e.g. change the colour or intensity of an LED strip) or take in the data to further process it and submit it to different channels (e.g. an emotion recognition algorithm which takes in image frames and send out timestamped JSON messages with emotion labels).
There is no direct point-to-point communication between producers and consumers of data. Instead, all communication happens in an asynchronous manner through publish-subscribe channels. This has the advantage of making all components in the system completely independent from one another. There should, however, be a method for service discovery, such that a new device joining the network can locate the central data relay and the type of communication channels that it supports.

4.3	Future Directions: 360° Camera Support (TUS)

The TUS AudiencePose system is designed for extensibility and continued development. A key area of future work is support for 360° camera inputs, which would enable comprehensive audience coverage from a single camera position—particularly valuable in immersive performance environments.

The current pipeline (YOLOv8 person detection, MediaPipe pose estimation) is trained on standard perspective imagery. To accommodate 360° equirectangular video, the planned approach is "virtual camera extraction": programmatically extracting multiple perspective views (e.g., front, left, right, rear) from the 360° feed, processing each view through the existing pipeline, and aggregating the engagement scores across all extracted views.

This approach leverages existing trained models without retraining, maintains real-time performance through parallel view processing, and provides a natural path to full-venue audience analysis. Implementation is estimated at 1-2 weeks of development effort using established libraries such as py360convert or OpenCV's equirectangular projection tools.
5	Conclusion (Ronan)
This deliverable presents the architecture for AMPLIFY PORTABLE, which is designed to meet the requirements of Pilot 1 and Pilot 2, Renewing Tradition and Music for Babies, respectively. The architecture is designed around a series of component blocks that are common to both pilots; the component blocks can be easily adapted to meet the specific requirements of each pilot. The architecture represents a solid foundation in the ongoing development of AMPLIFY PORTABLE.
The development and implementation of the AMPLIFY PORTABLE architecture will proceed over the coming months. The lessons learned from Pilot 0 by all parties involved played an important part in the successful development of the architecture presented in this document, they will continue to do so as the AMPLIFY project evolves.
